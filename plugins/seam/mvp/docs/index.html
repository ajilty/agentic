Seam

An overlay that reads your mail and calendar, works out what is actually asking something of you, and puts it on one page — where every claim links to its source and nothing acts in the world without your approval.

What Seam is

Your work lives in threads that cross surfaces. No single inbox can see the through-line, so you hold it in your head and things fall through the seams: an ask you made that never got answered, a decision buried in a chain, a deadline someone mentioned in passing.

Seam syncs those surfaces into a local store, infers the structure — messages to threads, threads to workstreams — and compiles a board of what needs you now. It holds only the delta between what is happening in your communications and what your formal systems already know. In perfect sync it holds nothing.

What it is not

  • Not a system of record. It never replaces your tracker or your wiki. It points at them.
  • Not an autonomous actor. You approve every write. This build has zero write access at all; when drafting and private calendar holds arrive, they will stage for your confirmation and never send, post, or notify anyone else. Narrow self-scoped rules (a mail filter affecting only you) may be earned later, per-item confirmed.
  • Not a team product. One user, one machine at a time. Nothing is shared, nothing is uploaded to a vendor.
  • Not an assistant that acts on your behalf. Every card ends at a link into the native app, where you do the thing.

Install & first run

Seam is a Claude Code plugin. It runs where your MCP connections already live, so it needs no new credentials of its own.

export SEAM_PROFILE=personal
node bin/seam.mjs setup       # the 10-minute seeding conversation — this is first run
node bin/seam.mjs sync        # first pass, seeded

Setup is a conversation, not a config file. It asks you to name three to five workstreams, point at their channels, and list the people you cannot keep waiting — then writes the profile for you. Day one arrives confident and pre-sorted rather than empty and apologetic. (The underlying YAML at ~/.seam/profiles/ is editable directly as the advanced path.)

Then open <store_root>/board/index.html. Sync regenerates it; the page is static.

Where your data sits

Everything Seam derives lives in plain files under store_root, a folder you choose. Raw messages as JSONL, workstreams and facts as markdown, state changes as an append-only ledger. No database, no service, no vendor. You can read all of it in a text editor and delete it with rm -rf.

Reading the board

Three modes share one spine. Operate is what needs you now, and is the whole of this build. Reflect (rollups over a window) and Know (durable facts held while your wiki lags) are designed but not yet built — they appear in the limits table below as later.

Each card is a compiled story, not a forwarded message:

ElementWhat it tells you
Type badgeThe shape of the next step — REPLY, REVIEW, NUDGE, APPROVE.
TitleThe recommended next action, phrased for outcome. ≤ 12 words.
WHY NOWThe urgency, with its evidence. ≤ 14 words.
CONTEXTThe situation in one line. ≤ 18 words.
Signal chipsExtracted evidence: dates, silence duration, corroboration, external origin.
N messagesThe underlying thread, each message deep-linked to its source.

Those word budgets are enforced by a linter, not a guideline. A card that cannot say why it matters in fourteen words is a card that does not know why it matters.

Tiers & provisional state

Three urgency tiers: Critical Now, Opportunity Now, Over the Horizon. Action type — reply, nudge, review, approve — is a lens you filter by, not a tier. "Waiting on someone" is one such action type and its items carry their own urgency like anything else; it is never a separate bucket.

Critical Now is soft-capped at five. Past five, Seam ranks and demotes with its reasoning visible ("demoted: due Friday, not today"). A Critical tier with twelve items in it means nothing, so the cap is a feature of the priority model rather than of the layout.

Anything Seam assigned itself renders provisional: dashed border, hollow chip. Tapping the chip confirms it in place and teaches the model. Confirmed items render solid. You should always be able to tell, at a glance, which judgments are yours and which are the machine's.

Over the Horizon never badges during the week. It surfaces at Review Friday, in one guided pass.

Claims & provenance

Every extracted signal is tappable and resolves to the exact message it came from. No claim without a link — if Seam says "due Wednesday" it will show you who said so and when.

Urgency from outside is marked

A deadline asserted by an external sender is labelled urgency claimed by sender — unverified and can never reach Critical Now on its own. It needs internal corroboration: a date in your tracker, a message from someone inside. A vendor's "expires Friday" is a sales tactic until something of yours agrees.

Cold start

Bottom-up inference needs history, and day one has none. Seam is explicit about it rather than pretending confidence:

  • Day one leads with the two things that work with zero inference — extracted dates, and asks of yours that got no reply.
  • Workstreams appear as proposals ("these nine messages look like one effort — name it?"). Unnamed proposals re-cluster on the next sync; naming one makes it stable.
  • A settling in banner replaces confident tiering until confirmation volume crosses a threshold.
  • The person filter stays off until the registry has enough addresses to be useful.

You can shortcut all of this with a ten-minute seeding conversation: name three to five workstreams, point at their channels, list the people you cannot keep waiting. Inference then corroborates and extends rather than bootstrapping from nothing.

Safety model

Seam moves text written by people outside your organisation toward an agent. That is the whole risk, and it is designed for rather than mitigated after.

RuleHow it is enforced
Synced content is data, never instructions designed The posture is settled and no compilation code exists yet to violate it. When it is built, untrusted text will be structurally separated from the instruction channel (not merely delimiter-wrapped, which is defeatable), and adversarial evals will report an injection rate.
You approve every write enforced today This build holds no write capability. The write allowlist in the profile is empty, and a repo lint greps the source for send-capable tool names. Honest scope of that lint: it checks our own source text — it is a naming guard, not a runtime capability boundary. The real boundary is the empty allowlist plus your per-write confirmation, and binding it to the host's tool manifest at call time is planned.
External-sourced facts never auto-promote designed The rule is settled and a fixture exists for the attacker-derived-fact case; the compiler and its golden test are not built yet. We will not claim this as enforced until the test runs.
External urgency never reaches Critical alone designed Visible in the board today ("held at opportunity"), but the tiering code and its golden are not written. Designed, not yet enforced.
Rules are never taken from message bodies designed The poisoned-message fixture exists; the adjudication and the injection_attempt_logged ledger event are specified and shown in the prototype ledger, not yet wired in code.
Residency enforced today Seam refuses to run if the store belongs to a different profile — real, and covered by a test. Scope limit: it compares a profile name to a store marker, not the account identity of the connected mailbox; binding to connector identity is planned.

What leaves your machine

Your messages live in plain files on your machine. But inference is not local: to compile a card, Seam sends the relevant message text to a model provider over the network. We say so plainly because the alternative — implying nothing ever leaves — is the claim that would fail an audit.

WhatWhere it goes
Message text needed to compile a card (sender, snippet, thread context) The model provider named in your profile, per compilation. Nothing else.
Raw messages, entities, ledger, learned state Nowhere. Plain files under your store_root. No server, no account, no telemetry.

Every model call is recorded in the ledger as a model_call event carrying the provider, the exact versioned model id (not a family name), the message ids included, and the bytes sent — so "what left, and when" is answerable line by line, not asserted.

What the provider does with it

Seam is provider-agnostic by design: you choose the model provider and bring your own agreement with them. Naming the provider is half the disclosure; the other half is their handling terms, which are yours to set, not ours to assert:

QuestionAnswer (from provider terms)
Retention window for submitted content<fill from DPA>
Is submitted content used for training?<fill from DPA>
DPA / BAA in place?<fill>
Data-residency region<fill>

Until these are filled for your provider, treat Seam as unfit for regulated mail. The seeding conversation also sends content (workstream and people names); it is covered by the same terms.

The ledger

Every state change — yours and Seam's — appends to events/ledger-YYYY-MM.jsonl with an actor attached.

{"ts":"2026-08-07T07:42:08Z","actor":"agent","type":"model_call",
 "provider":"anthropic","model":"claude",
 "message_ids":["email:m-5001a"],"bytes_sent":2841}
{"ts":"2026-08-07T07:42:09Z","actor":"agent","type":"tier_assigned",
 "action":"act_201","tier":"critical",
 "reason":"internal corroboration + extracted deadline"}
{"ts":"2026-08-07T08:03:44Z","actor":"user","type":"tier_confirmed",
 "action":"act_205"}

Which means "what did this thing do while I was away" is answerable completely, in a file you own, in a format you can grep. Honest limit: it is a plaintext log the writing process could in principle rewrite — a debug-grade audit trail, not a tamper-evident one. Hash-chaining or an append-only sink is the planned hardening; today the value is completeness and legibility, not cryptographic integrity.

Freshness & dark sources

A stale board that looks fresh is worse than no board. Each source carries its own freshness chip. If one fails, it goes dark: its chip turns red, its signals pause, and the sync completes anyway — one broken connection never blanks the page.

Silence maths excludes dark windows

"Four days silent" is a lie if the source was not syncing for two of them. Seam subtracts dark windows before it tells you nobody answered.

Profiles

One install, many personas. A profile binds a persona to its store, its MCP servers, its real tool names, and its people.

Lives inHoldsTravels by
The pluginCode, prompts, adapters, fixtures, the board templategit
~/.seam/profiles/Your real bindings, store root, principals Nothing — per machine, never committed
The storeMessages, entities, ledger, what Seam has learnedYour own folder sync

Prompts and logic address abstract surfaces — email, chat, calendar — never products. Supporting a new provider is a new adapter and fixtures, with no prompt changes.

The one thing to understand before you rely on it

Seam proposes. You dispose. It cannot act in the world without your approval — today it cannot act at all — and every judgment it makes is labelled as its own until you confirm it. If that ever stops being true, the ledger will show you.
